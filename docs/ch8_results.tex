\section{Experiment Harness}

The evaluation is performed with the experiment harness of the system. An experiment is created with \code{POST /experiments} and is described by a name, a tracker, a scenario and a number of frames. The harness runs the experiment, stores its status and metrics in the \code{Experiment} record, and allows the results to be downloaded as JSON or CSV with \code{GET /experiments/{id}/export}. The metrics computed by the \code{AnalyticsEngine} are IDF1, HOTA and the number of ID switches.

\section{Detector Selection}

The first experiment measured on real data is the detector itself, because the training
runs were logged by Ultralytics and their validation metrics are therefore available
without re-training. Four training runs are recorded in the repository under
\code{models/}: a YOLOv8n run of 50 epochs, two YOLOv8m runs (10 and 93 epochs) and a
YOLOv8x run of 40 epochs. Table~\ref{tab:detector} lists, for each run, the epoch with
the best validation \code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}}, the precision and recall at that
epoch, the size of the exported weights and the inference latency measured locally on a
640-pixel test mosaic. Figure~\ref{fig:detmap} plots the validation
\code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} against the epoch for the two longest runs.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\hr \textbf{Run} & \textbf{Ep.} & \textbf{P} & \textbf{R} & \textbf{mAP\textsubscript{50}} & \textbf{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} & \textbf{ms/frame}\\
\midrule
\hr \code{mango\_yolov8n-4} & 49 & 0.946 & 0.975 & 0.985 & \textbf{0.827} & \textbf{33.6}\\
\hr \code{mango\_yolov8m\_exp-2} & 10 & 0.928 & 0.949 & 0.979 & 0.808 & 83.5\\

\bottomrule

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{detector_mAP.png}
\caption[Detector validation curves]{Validation \code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} per epoch for

\label{fig:detmap}
\end{figure}

The smallest model is the best choice for this system: it reaches the highest
\code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} of all four runs (0.827), it is the fastest at 33.6\,ms per
frame, and its weights are 6.2\,MB against 52\,MB and 546\,MB for the medium and large
models. It is therefore the default detector of the deployment. The caveat is that the
runs are not a controlled ablation: the two YOLOv8m runs and the YOLOv8n run were trained
on a second, larger annotation set and for different numbers of epochs, and the YOLOv8x
run was logged only at its final epoch. The comparison therefore mixes architecture,
dataset and schedule, and it is used here to justify the deployment default, not to make a
claim about the intrinsic accuracy of the three architectures.

\section{Discussion}

\add{The discussion should interpret the numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:trackers} and \ref{tab:cam}: which tracker keeps identities longest through occlusion, whether camera-motion compensation reduces identity switches, how the ReID threshold trades merged identities against fragmented ones, and how the runtime per frame compares with the video frame rate. It should also include a short analysis of failure cases, such as fruit hidden for longer than the buffer, clusters of touching fruit, and fruit entering and leaving at the image border, supported by the examples in Figures~\ref{fig:reid} and \ref{fig:ui_tracks}.}
