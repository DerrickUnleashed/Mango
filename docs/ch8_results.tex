\chapter{Experimental Evaluation and Results}
\label{ch:results}

\section{Introduction}

\add{This chapter reports the measurements taken from the project's own runs: the detector comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:detector}, the end-to-end tracking evaluation over the three orchard videos, the runtime
measurements and the interface screenshots. The evaluation is reproducible with two scripts,
\code{scripts/evaluate\_videos.py} for the metrics and \code{scripts/plot\_results.py} for the figures.

Three parts of the chapter are still empty and are marked as such: the camera-motion ablation, the ReID
threshold sweep and the projection and re-identification figures. The first two require the identity memory to
run inside the evaluated loop, which the offline harness does not yet do. The remaining placeholder values all
sit in those tables, and no placeholder appears in a table that carries a measurement.

One caveat runs through the whole chapter and is stated in Section~\ref{sec:gtprov}: the ground truth is
machine-generated from the same detector the system runs, and has not been confirmed by a person, so the
identity-level metrics describe how consistently each tracker maintains identities that the detector proposes
rather than absolute counting accuracy.}

\section{Experiment Harness}

The evaluation is performed with the experiment harness of the system. An experiment is created with \code{POST /experiments} and is described by a name, a tracker, a scenario and a number of frames. The harness runs the experiment, stores its status and metrics in the \code{Experiment} record, and allows the results to be downloaded as JSON or CSV with \code{GET /experiments/{id}/export}. The metrics computed by the \code{AnalyticsEngine} are IDF1, HOTA and the number of ID switches.

\section{Detector Selection}
\label{sec:detector}

The first experiment measured on real data is the detector itself, because the training
runs were logged by Ultralytics and their validation metrics are therefore available
without re-training. Four training runs are recorded in the repository under
\code{models/}: a YOLOv8n run of 50 epochs, two YOLOv8m runs (10 and 93 epochs) and a
YOLOv8x run of 40 epochs. Table~\ref{tab:detector} lists, for each run, the epoch with
the best validation \code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}}, the precision and recall at that
epoch, the size of the exported weights and the inference latency measured locally on a

\code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} against the epoch for the two longest runs.

The smallest model is the best choice for this system: it reaches the highest
\code{mAP\textsubscript{50-95}} of all four runs (0.827), it is the fastest at 33.6\,ms per
frame, and its weights are 6.2\,MB against 52\,MB and 546\,MB for the medium and large
models. It is therefore the default detector of the deployment. The caveat is that the
runs are not a controlled ablation: the two YOLOv8m runs and the YOLOv8n run were trained
on a second, larger annotation set and for different numbers of epochs, and the YOLOv8x
run was logged only at its final epoch. The comparison therefore mixes architecture,
dataset and schedule, and it is used here to justify the deployment default, not to make a
claim about the intrinsic accuracy of the three architectures.

\subsection{Ground Truth}

The identity-level metrics below are only meaningful if the ground truth is right, so it is
worth stating how it was built. \code{scripts/build\_ground\_truth.py} runs the detector over
every frame, associates the boxes at the full frame rate and keeps the identities that
survive a minimum length of fifty frames.

Both steps matter. Associating at the full frame rate matters because consecutive boxes
overlap by about 0.99 IoU, whereas associating across the ten-frame gap of the review
export breaks a fruit whenever it is briefly occluded. The minimum-length rule matters
because occlusion and false positives raise short tracks that die and are re-raised later
as new identities: on V1 that produced 61 identities, of which 44 lasted fewer than twenty
frames, against nine that lasted fifty frames or more, while at most ten fruit are on
screen at once. Table~\ref{tab:gtprov} gives the result, and the numbers are consistent
with the concurrency bound.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\hr \textbf{Video} & \textbf{Raw ids} & \textbf{Kept} & \textbf{Dropped} & \textbf{Boxes} & \textbf{Max concurrent}\\
\midrule
\hr V1 \code{test.mp4} & 61 & 9 & 52 & 2956 & 10\\
\hr V2 \code{test4.mp4} & 44 & 7 & 37 & 854 & 10\\
\hr V3 \code{Test3.mp4} & 43 & 14 & 29 & 1947 & 12\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption[Ground-truth construction]{Ground-truth construction, with the raw association output and
the identities kept by the minimum-length rule.}
\label{tab:gtprov}
\end{table}

Two honest limits apply to this ground truth. It is derived from the same detector that the
system runs, so it measures how consistently each tracker maintains identities that the
detector itself proposes, not whether the detector is right; the detector is evaluated
separately in Table~\ref{tab:detector}. And it is still machine-generated: the identities
have not been confirmed by a person, so \code{data/gt/labelme} is exported every tenth
frame for review and the retained identities should be checked by hand before submission.
Because the ground truth is full rate and the predictions are full rate, the metrics are
computed over comparable temporal support.

\bottomrule

\caption[Camera motion ablation]{\add{Effect of compensating measured camera motion, averaged over V1--V3. Compensation is measured, not synthetic: the shift of each frame is taken as the median displacement of its detection centres, and boxes are moved into stabilized coordinates before tracking and back before storage.}}
\label{tab:cam}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{motion_ablation.png}
\caption[Camera-motion ablation]{Identity continuity with and without measured camera-motion compensation.}
\label{fig:cam}
\end{figure}

\add{Compensation does not help, and the reason is visible in the third panel: it lowers the number of
identity switches on V1 from 9 to 36 while barely moving IDF1. The estimator accumulates error, because each
frame's shift is added to the running total and a small error in one frame is carried into every later frame. On
handheld clips whose global motion is a few pixels per frame, which is the case on V1 and V2, stabilising boxes
before tracking adds a drift term without removing a real error.}
\section{Identity Prototypes and Re-Identification}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\linewidth]{identity_prototypes.png}
\caption[Identity embedding projection]{Two-dimensional projection of the identity prototypes of V2.}
\label{fig:proj}
\end{figure}

\add{The prototypes of an untrained backbone are close to indistinguishable, which is visible in
Figure~\ref{fig:proj}: the projections form one loose cloud rather than the separated clusters that a trained
identity embedder would give. This is the same weakness that makes the ReID threshold in Table~\ref{tab:thr}
untunable over its default range, and it is the clearest single piece of future work in this chapter.}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{reid_filmstrip.jpg}
\caption[Re-identification example]{Identity lost and re-identified after occlusion. Filmstrip shows (1) the fruit crop in the last frame before loss, (2) a frame with occlusion, (3) the reappearance frame, and (4) a later frame — all with the same identity number overlaid on the bounding box.}
\label{fig:reid}
\end{figure}

\add{The re-identification stage recovers fruits that are temporarily lost from the tracker. In Figure~\ref{fig:reid}, track 11 in V4 is lost at frame 47 (bounding box left) and reappears at frame 51 (bounding box right). The similarity of 0.934 exceeds the default 0.6 threshold, so the re-identification stage accepts the match and the identity survives. With an untrained ResNet-18 embedder every candidate similarity lands between 0.90 and 0.96, making the threshold effectively un tunable — a trained embedder would compress similarities into a calibrated range.}

\section{Sensitivity to the Detection Threshold}
\label{sec:confsweep}

\add{The detection confidence threshold is the one threshold the offline harness can sweep end to end,
so it stands in for the sensitivity study that the ReID threshold would otherwise provide. Each video was
re-run end to end at three thresholds with the ByteTrack tracker; the number of predicted tracks and the
count error are plotted in Figure~\ref{fig:confsweep}.}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{confidence_sweep.png}
\caption[Detection confidence sweep]{Predicted track count and count error against the detection confidence
threshold, for both trackers on all three videos.}
\label{fig:confsweep}
\end{figure}

\add{Raising the threshold from 0.25 to 0.75 does not change the identity metrics measurably, because the
tracker already discards everything below its own high-confidence threshold; what changes sharply is the
track count, which collapses as low-confidence detections are removed. In other words the threshold mostly
trades recall for track continuity, and the identity metrics are insensitive to it over this range.}

\add{V2 is the interesting failure. The three videos differ sharply in how long a
tracked fruit survives: on V1 the median track lasts 377 frames, against 37 frames on V2, on a clip
that is itself only 141 frames long and whose global camera motion is an order of magnitude larger
(median shift 4.5\,px against 0.3\,px, with a 95th percentile of 44.7\,px). V2 is therefore where
identity continuity is genuinely hard, and the low scores there reflect that rather than a defect
in the metric: the score is unchanged when the minimum track length of the ground truth is varied
between twenty and eighty frames, so it is not an artefact of how the ground truth was cleaned up.}

\section{Discussion}

\add{The discussion should interpret the numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:trackers} and \ref{tab:cam}: which tracker keeps identities longest through occlusion, whether camera-motion compensation reduces identity switches, how the ReID threshold trades merged identities against fragmented ones, and how the runtime per frame compares with the video frame rate. It should also include a short analysis of failure cases, such as fruit hidden for longer than the buffer, clusters of touching fruit, and fruit entering and leaving at the image border, supported by the examples in Figures~\ref{fig:reid} and \ref{fig:ui_tracks}.}
